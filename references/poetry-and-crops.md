# Poetry and crop direction

Use this guide when selecting fragments or writing the poem.

## Read the photograph

Privately note five kinds of evidence before writing:

- **Subject:** who or what carries the scene.
- **Motion:** an actual or implied action such as leaning, drifting, waiting, folding, dissolving, or returning.
- **Material:** one or two tactile details such as glass, wet pavement, linen, smoke, bark, skin, metal, or reflected light.
- **Time/weather:** only what the image supports.
- **Tension:** a quiet contrast such as closeness/distance, warmth/cold, stillness/motion, concealment/revelation, or ordinary/strange.

Build the poem from observed evidence, not a generic mood label.

## Write with restraint

- Default to English. Use another language only when the user explicitly requests it.
- Write one sentence or sentence-like poetic clause of 12–24 words. Use one terminal punctuation mark at most.
- Treat it as one continuous poem. Supply no manual line breaks by default; let the renderer wrap it into 1–3 centered lines.
- Prefer one fresh relation between concrete things over several decorative adjectives.
- Let the fragment act as a visual noun, modifier, interruption, or piece of evidence. Place it directly beside the phrase it enriches.
- The surrounding syntax should remain intentional if the bracketed fragment is read as a silent image.
- Use one governing metaphor at most. Leave interpretive space.
- Use correct, natural language; poetry is not an excuse for arbitrary fragments.

Avoid:

- stock phrases such as “岁月静好”, “人间值得”, “治愈一切”, “奔赴山海”, “时光荏苒”, “在光影里”, “温柔了岁月”, “stories untold”, “dance of life”, or “whispers of time”;
- unsupported emotion, biography, symbolism, season, or location;
- florid synonym stacking, motivational conclusions, hashtags, titles, signatures, dates, and quotation marks around the whole poem;
- literal inventory sentences that merely list visible objects;
- wording that names every fragment and leaves no visual participation for the viewer.
- separate caption-like lines, parallel slogans, or four short statements stacked vertically;
- poems that need more than three visual lines at the default scale.

## Select fragments

Choose 3–6 regions with distinct semantic roles, for example:

- a focal detail: eye catchlight, flower head, flame, bird, hand-held object;
- a material clue: fabric fold, reflection, painted surface, window condensation;
- a directional clue: branch tip, gaze, hand, road edge, shadow, wake;
- a color or light counterpoint;
- a small contextual fragment that makes the main subject less obvious and more poetic.

Selection rules:

- Choose the lower-panel cover focus first. All regions must lie fully inside that visible cover crop.
- Use varied rectangles, usually 1:1, 4:5, 5:4, 2:3, or 3:2. Keep their displayed long side roughly 65–145 px on a 1536 × 2048 canvas so fragments punctuate the sentence without overpowering it.
- Preserve enough surrounding pixels for each fragment to read; do not crop only an indistinguishable color field.
- Do not repeat almost identical fragments unless repetition is the photograph's defining rhythm.
- Keep a clear visual gap between lower voids. Total void area should normally remain below 12% of the lower panel.
- Avoid the image edges, caption areas, UI chrome, watermarks, and pre-existing blank rectangles.

## Typography and rhythm

- Treat words, brackets, and image fragments as one calm typographic field.
- Prefer sentence case and a monospaced mechanical typewriter face with real-ink rendering. Default to 36 px at 1536 × 2048; stay within 34–40 px unless the user asks otherwise.
- Center each line and center the combined poem block vertically in the upper half. The block should feel like one sentence floating in calm negative space.
- Prefer 2–3 naturally wrapped lines. One line is acceptable for a very short poem; four or more lines are not.
- Use moderate line spacing, roughly 0.55–0.65 times the font size in addition to each line's content height.
- Do not bold keywords or vary font families, colors, or sizes within the poem. Apply one restrained, consistent print impression to words and brackets alike.
- Use one bracket pair throughout: `(` `)` by default, `{` `}` only when the user requests the reference's alternate treatment.
- A fragment group must never be stranded alone on a line unless the entire composition deliberately resolves to that single image.
