# Natural cinematic film direction

Use this guide to keep the lower photograph refined and filmic without turning it into a conspicuous filter.

## Default visual target

Aim for the restraint of a well-timed contemporary film scan:

- a gentle highlight shoulder that keeps bright windows, lamps, clouds, and skin highlights from feeling digitally harsh;
- a soft toe with readable shadow detail rather than crushed black areas;
- slightly restrained saturation while protecting distinctive source colors;
- subtly cool shadow neutrals and warm highlights, with no obvious orange-and-teal split;
- a faint optical vignette that guides attention without looking like a preset;
- fine monochromatic grain that is noticed only at full size or on close inspection.

The result should still be recognizably the uploaded photograph. The treatment is a global color-and-tone finish, not a restyle.

## Strength decisions

- Use the default `cinematic` setting for ordinary requests.
- Lower `strength` to roughly `0.45–0.60` for already graded movie stills, very dark night scenes, delicate pastel images, or photographs with intentionally neutral product color.
- Raise `strength` only to roughly `0.80–0.88` when a clean digital source feels unusually clinical. Do not exceed `1.0`.
- Keep `grain` around `0.02–0.04`. Use the low end for faces, skies, compression-heavy images, and small originals. Use the high end only for clean, high-resolution files.
- Turn the look off only when the user explicitly asks to preserve the original color rendering or requests no filter.

## Protect the photograph

- Judge exposure and color from the whole fitted lower panel, not from a single crop.
- Preserve natural skin hue and tonal separation. Never use the film look to beautify or smooth a person.
- Keep highlights textured and shadows legible; the grade should not hide source defects by making the image darker or hazier.
- Do not add dust, scratches, gate weave, sprocket holes, light leaks, date stamps, faux borders, chromatic aberration, or other nostalgic effects.
- Apply the grade before extracting poem fragments so every fragment matches the lower photograph exactly.

## Final inspection

Compare the finished lower panel with the source at useful size. Revise when the filter is the first thing noticed, when skin shifts orange or gray, when neutral architecture turns strongly cyan, when blacks lose information, or when grain competes with facial and fabric detail.
